Module: `trainhorn_calc.electrical`. CLI: `wire`.

A horn compressor draws tens of amps at 12 V. Thin wire wastes part of that
voltage as heat, the compressor slows down and the tank takes longer to fill.
`suggest_awg` picks the **smallest copper AWG size** whose voltage drop stays
within a budget (3 % by default).

## The voltage-drop method

Current flows out to the load and back, so the conductor length is **twice the
one-way run**:

```text
V_drop = I · R_per_length · 2 · L_one_way
drop_% = V_drop / V_system · 100
```

`suggest_awg` walks the common automotive and battery-cable sizes from thin to
thick (18, 16, 14, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1, 1/0, 2/0, 3/0, 4/0) and returns the
first one with `drop_% ≤ max_drop_pct`. If even 4/0 cannot meet the budget, it
raises `InputError`.

<Info>
A chassis ground return is not free. It is usually worse than a dedicated
wire, so the library counts both directions as copper.
</Info>

## Copper resistance table

The resistances are **computed**, not copied from a table:

- **AWG diameter** (ASTM B258): `d(n) = 0.005 in · 92^((36 - n) / 39)`, where
  1/0, 2/0, 3/0 and 4/0 use n = 0, -1, -2, -3.
- **Resistivity** of annealed copper at 20 °C, 100 % IACS (International
  Annealed Copper Standard): `ρ = 1.7241e-8 Ω·m`.
- **Resistance**: `R = ρ · L / A`, with `A = π·d²/4`.
- **Temperature** (optional): `R_T = R_20 · (1 + 0.00393 · (T - 20))`.

The computed values agree with published solid-copper AWG tables (for example
the NIST Copper Wire Tables, Handbook 100) to within about 1 %. Stranded wire
of the same gauge is a few percent higher, which the default 3 % budget
absorbs.

## Worked 12 V example

A compressor draws **30 A** and sits **10 ft** (one way) from the battery in a
**12 V** system. Budget: **3 %**, which is 0.36 V.

1. Round trip: 2 × 10 = 20 ft of copper.
2. Try 8 AWG: 0.6282 Ω/1000 ft × 20 ft = 0.012564 Ω; × 30 A = 0.377 V, which is
   3.14 % of 12 V. Too much.
3. Try 6 AWG: 0.3951 Ω/1000 ft × 20 ft = 0.007902 Ω; × 30 A = 0.237 V, which is
   1.98 %. Within budget.

```console
$ trainhorn-calc wire 30 10
6 AWG copper: 0.24 V drop (1.98% of 12 V, limit 3%)
```

```python
from trainhorn_calc import suggest_awg, voltage_drop

s = suggest_awg(amps=30, one_way_length=10, system_voltage=12)
s.awg                     # '6'
s.voltage_drop_v          # 0.23703872341214613
s.voltage_drop_pct        # 1.9753226951012177
voltage_drop(30, 10, "8") # 0.3769063337935234  (3.14 %, over budget)
```

### Variations

```console
$ trainhorn-calc wire 30 10 --volts 24
10 AWG copper: 0.60 V drop (2.50% of 24 V, limit 3%)

$ trainhorn-calc wire 30 10 --max-drop 5
10 AWG copper: 0.60 V drop (4.99% of 12 V, limit 5%)

$ trainhorn-calc wire 30 10 --temp-c 80
6 AWG copper: 0.29 V drop (2.44% of 12 V, limit 3%)

$ trainhorn-calc wire 15 15
8 AWG copper: 0.28 V drop (2.36% of 12 V, limit 3%)

$ trainhorn-calc wire 20 3 --unit m
8 AWG copper: 0.25 V drop (2.06% of 12 V, limit 3%)
```

- At **24 V** the same current and run need only 10 AWG: the allowed drop in
  volts doubles.
- A **hot engine bay** raises resistance. At 80 °C, 6 AWG is 0.4882 Ω per
  1000 ft instead of 0.3951, and the drop grows from 1.98 % to 2.44 %.
- Lengths can be in metres with `--unit m` / `unit="m"`.

When no size works:

```console
$ trainhorn-calc wire 400 50
error: no gauge up to 4/0 keeps the drop within 3% (400 A over 50 ft); shorten the run, raise the system voltage or use parallel conductors
```

The command exits with status 2.

## Scope and safety

- This method checks **voltage drop only**. It does not check ampacity
  (insulation temperature rating, bundling, engine-bay heat).
- **Size the fuse to protect the wire you choose**, and place it close to the
  battery.
- Use the compressor's continuous current draw, not its fuse rating, as `amps`.
