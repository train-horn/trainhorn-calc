## Free-air volume (Boyle's law)

Horns and compressors are rated in **CFM of free air**: cubic feet per minute
measured at atmospheric pressure. A tank, on the other hand, is a fixed volume
of compressed air. To connect the two, convert what the tank holds into free
air.

At constant temperature, Boyle's law says `P·V` is constant for a given mass
of air. A tank of volume `V_tank` at absolute pressure `P_abs` therefore holds
as much air as `V_tank·P_abs / P_atm` at atmospheric pressure.

Gauges read **gauge** pressure (psig), so `P_abs = P_gauge + P_atm`, with
`P_atm` = 14.696 psi at sea level. When the tank drops from `P_start` to
`P_end` (both gauge), the atmospheric part cancels and the free air released is

```text
V_free = V_tank · (P_start - P_end) / P_atm
```

The horn time and the refill time then follow directly:

```text
horn_seconds     = V_free / horn_CFM       · 60
recovery_seconds = V_free / compressor_CFM · 60
```

1 US gallon is 231 in³, which is 0.13368 ft³
(`CUBIC_FEET_PER_GALLON = 0.13368055555555555`).

## Worked example: 5-gallon tank

A 5-gallon tank is filled to 150 psi. The horn still sounds acceptable down to
110 psi and uses 10 CFM of free air. The compressor is off while you blow
(worst case).

1. Tank volume: 5 × 0.13368 = 0.6684 ft³.
2. Pressure drop: 150 - 110 = 40 psi.
3. Free air: 0.6684 × 40 / 14.696 = 1.8193 ft³.
4. Horn time: 1.8193 / 10 × 60 = 10.92 s.

The library gives the same answer:

```console
$ trainhorn-calc runtime 5 150 110 10 --blast 2
10.9 s of horn time (1.82 cu ft of free air); 5 blasts of 2 s

$ trainhorn-calc runtime 5 150 110 10 --json
{
  "free_air_cuft": 1.8192781104457751,
  "runtime_seconds": 10.91566866267465
}
```

What changes the answer:

```console
$ trainhorn-calc runtime 2.5 150 110 10 --blast 1
5.5 s of horn time (0.91 cu ft of free air); 5 blasts of 1 s

$ trainhorn-calc runtime 5 150 90 10
16.4 s of horn time (2.73 cu ft of free air)

$ trainhorn-calc runtime 5 150 110 10 --atm-psi 12.2
13.1 s of horn time (2.19 cu ft of free air)
```

- Halving the tank halves the runtime.
- Accepting a lower cut-off (90 instead of 110 psi) adds 50 % more usable
  pressure range, so 50 % more horn time.
- At altitude the ambient pressure is lower (about 12.2 psi near 5,000 ft), so
  each psi of gauge drop releases more free air. Pass `--atm-psi` /
  `atmospheric_psi` to account for it.

## Compressor recovery

The compressor switches on at the **cut-in** pressure and off at the
**cut-out** pressure. Refilling a 2-gallon tank from 110 to 150 psi with a
compressor that delivers 1.0 CFM of free air:

```console
$ trainhorn-calc recovery 2 110 150 1.0
43.7 s to refill from 110 to 150 psi

$ trainhorn-calc recovery 5 110 150 1.0
109.2 s to refill from 110 to 150 psi
```

### Output that falls with pressure

A compressor delivers less air as the tank pressure rises. Use the CFM the
maker rates at (or near) your cut-out pressure for the simple model; a 0 psi
rating gives an optimistic time.

If you know both figures, the library models delivery as falling linearly from
`cfm_at_zero_psi` at 0 psi to `compressor_cfm` at `rated_psi`:
`q(p) = a + b·p`. Integrating `dt = (V_tank / P_atm)·dp / q(p)` gives

```text
t = V_tank / P_atm · ln( q(p_out) / q(p_in) ) / b · 60      (b ≠ 0)
```

```console
$ trainhorn-calc recovery 2 110 150 1.0 --cfm-at-zero 2.0 --rated-psi 150
38.7 s to refill from 110 to 150 psi
```

Here the compressor makes 2.0 CFM at 0 psi and 1.0 CFM at 150 psi. Between
110 and 150 psi it averages a little more than 1.0 CFM, so the refill is
shorter than the constant 1.0 CFM estimate (38.7 s instead of 43.7 s).

### A full blow-and-refill cycle

With the 5-gallon tank above and a 1.0 CFM compressor: 10.9 s of horn time
empties the tank to 110 psi, and the compressor needs 109.2 s to get back to
150 psi. Plan on roughly two minutes between full-length sequences, or a
larger compressor.

## Python API

```python
from trainhorn_calc import (
    free_air_volume_cuft, tank_runtime_seconds, blast_count,
    compressor_recovery_seconds,
)

free_air_volume_cuft(5, 150, 110)                     # 1.8192781104457751
secs = tank_runtime_seconds(5, 150, 110, 10)          # 10.91566866267465
blast_count(secs, 2)                                  # 5
compressor_recovery_seconds(2, 110, 150, 1.0)         # 43.662674650698605
compressor_recovery_seconds(2, 110, 150, 1.0,
                            cfm_at_zero_psi=2.0,
                            rated_psi=150)            # 38.70512365385508
```

## Assumptions and limits

- **Isothermal expansion.** Fast blasts cool the air, so real runtime is a
  little shorter. Treat results as an upper estimate.
- **Constant horn consumption** at every tank pressure above the cut-off, with
  the compressor off while the horn sounds.
- **No losses** in lines, valves, fittings or leaks.
- The compressor duty cycle and thermal limits are not modelled. Check the
  maker's duty-cycle rating before you plan back-to-back refills.
